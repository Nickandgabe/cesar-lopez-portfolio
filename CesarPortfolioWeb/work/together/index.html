Overview

An intelligent navigator for an overwhelming system.

Together translates the U.S. healthcare and education systems — IEPs, insurance mandates, therapy options — into a single, culturally relevant, bilingual space designed around what families actually need.

Project N1 began as a centralized digital ecosystem for parents in the U.S. with children on the autism spectrum. Its job is to act as an intelligent navigator — translating the complexity of IEPs, insurance mandates, and state-specific Medicaid waivers into accessible, culturally relevant language.

Beyond information, the platform fosters a support community connecting families with specialists in functional medicine, nutrition, and neurodiversity, plus a personalized tracking tool so parents can document progress and protocol responses — creating a data-driven bridge between home life and clinical care.

The result, live at alwaystogheter.com, is Together: three pillars — Learn, Find ABA Centers, and Connect — built into one warm, bilingual product.

The Problem

The system wasn't built in a family's language.

The need for Together is underscored by real disparities in U.S. healthcare access — disparities that hit Hispanic families hardest.

The goal: less time coordinating care, more time being a family.

Diagnosed later, served less

Per the CDC (2023), autism prevalence is similar across ethnic groups (~1 in 36 children) — yet Hispanic children are diagnosed later and face greater barriers to specialized services.

An administrative maze

Language gaps compound the "administrative burden" of navigating state-specific Medicaid waivers and IDEA — a maze most families face without a guide.

Isolation, not agency

Up to 95% of children with ASD experience co-occurring conditions like GI issues or sleep disorders — yet care stays fragmented and families are left to coordinate it alone.

Process

From market gap to a product families trust.

The design challenge was tonal as much as structural: build something clinically credible without ever feeling clinical.

01

Research

Grounded the product in CDC data and the real gap between Hispanic families and specialized autism care in the U.S.

02

Structure

Distilled a complex problem space into three clear pillars: Learn, Find ABA Centers, and Connect — each solving one part of the burden.

03

Design

Chose a warm, illustrated, rounded visual language on purpose — the opposite of a sterile healthcare portal — and built it bilingual from the ground up.

04

Build

Shipped a responsive, component-based front end with a live EN/ES toggle, a searchable provider directory, and a family-matching flow.

Built for the fastest-growing demographic in the U.S.

Every surface — navigation, resources, provider profiles — ships in English and Spanish from a single toggle. It's not a translated afterthought; it's core to Project N1's thesis: Hispanic cultural competency is part of the care, not an add-on.
